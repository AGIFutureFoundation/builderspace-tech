BuilderSpace · Why Louisiana

The build is arriving in Louisiana. The on-ramp should arrive with it.

BuilderSpace brings the coding and startup world from San Francisco / Silicon Valley to the towns on the tour — a new tech region — to empower them, match builders with potential employers, and bridge the tech divide, through events, hackathons, and builder days. Louisiana is the tour's first and largest leg because nowhere else does the arrival — and the distance a local builder has to travel to meet it — show up in the same parish.

The build arriving

Louisiana is in the middle of the largest industrial build-out in its history, and its centre of gravity is not the usual tech map — it is the rural northeast, the northwest corridor, and the southwest energy coast. The figures below are the announced figures, with their sources and their categories kept separate, the way this site always prints them: construction jobs and permanent jobs are different things, and they are never added together here.

Richland Parish — the Meta build

Meta's data centre between Rayville and Delhi, about 30 miles east of Monroe, was expanded in July 2026 to more than $50 billion invested, with over 7,500 construction workers at peak and 1,000 operational roles once the full build-out is operational (Meta). More than $1.6 billion is already contracted with Louisiana businesses, and the general contractors on the build are named and hiring.

Northwest Louisiana — the Amazon build

Amazon has committed $18 billion to data centres in northwest Louisiana, with up to 750 direct full-time jobs, plus construction employment counted separately (Amazon). Separately again, Amazon's Shreveport fulfilment centre already employs more than 2,000 people today — a working technology floor, not a future promise.

Southwest Louisiana — the energy coast

Three liquefied natural gas projects are past final investment decision, carrying very large construction demand over the coming years and permanent workforces in the hundreds at each terminal (company and state economic-development announcements). The pattern matches the rest of the state: the work is real, it is scheduled, and most of it sits outside the metro areas the tech world usually visits.

And the distance: Richland Parish — at the centre of the largest of these builds — records a poverty rate of 26.4% and a broadband subscription rate of 77.6%, the lowest connectivity of any stop on the route (U.S. Census Bureau). Across Louisiana, fibre reaches 30.8% of rural locations against 52.9% of urban ones (Federal Communications Commission map data, June 2025). The employers are coming. The question this leg of the tour exists to answer is whether local people get a fair on-ramp — skills, practice, and a guided walk to the door of the programmes that are already hiring.

The pipelines exist. The on-ramp is what's missing.

Louisiana is not starting from zero, and this page will not pretend otherwise. Louisiana Delta Community College runs a roughly four-week Data Center Technician course whose inaugural class saw all 11 graduates receive offers from a Meta-build contractor, and which was graduating about 24 people a month by October 2026. Meta has funded full scholarships into data-centre trade certificates for every Richland Parish high-school graduate from the class of 2026 onward. Meta's America's Workforce Academy — free, paid-to-learn, carrying Meta's own job guarantee for its graduates with Meta partners, on Meta's terms — has its Louisiana training centre in Baton Rouge.

What nobody is running is the layer before all of that: discovery in the towns themselves. A builder identity for a 19-year-old in Delhi or a career-changer in Monroe. Hands-on practice that builds pride before it builds a credential. An evening where a resident splices fibre, racks a server, and meets the contractor hiring for exactly that work — before they ever fill in a college application. The pipelines are strong and they start at the college door. BuilderSpace starts in the town and walks people to that door. The tour grants no credential and promises no one a job; it fills the top of the funnel and hands people to the institutions that do the rest.

What a Louisiana stop looks like

Builder days

Hands-on stations — fibre splicing and structured cabling, wiring, controls, rack-and-stack practice — led by instructors and working technicians. Every attendee leaves holding a result: a tested splice, a working build, a thing they made.

Hackathons where software fits

Build weekends themed on the town's real economy — data-centre operations, fibre and networks, energy, fulfilment and automation, main-street technology — with teams formed across towns, schools, trades, and ages on purpose.

Employer-matching sessions

Named employers, named roles, named next steps — in rooms, in person. See the Employer Partner page for how the matching is designed.

Both doors into the trades

Every stop presents the union route (registered apprenticeship information) and the non-union route (fast-track academy intake) side by side, named and neither favoured — because both doors into this build are formally open.

A next step with a name on it

Every event ends with each attendee holding a named next step and a named human — a college cohort intake, an academy application, an apprenticeship information session — and a follow-up from that human within 48 hours.

Food, music, and the town itself

Local cooks and local musicians are programming, not decoration. The town's culture and the new trades are the same line of people, and a Louisiana stop is run that way from the first flyer.

Universities and colleges — the partnership proposal

A university partner gives a stop three things no travelling team can carry: rooms where a hundred builders may legally and comfortably gather, credibility with the families and employers of its parish, and the student pipeline the employers are already hiring from. No institution below is a BuilderSpace partner today. Each entry is a target and a proposal — what the tour would ask, and what it would offer — and no institution's name appears in tour materials as a partner until that institution agrees in writing.

Louisiana Delta Community College — Monroe

Proposal. The tour proposes co-hosted builder days in Monroe feeding the Data Center Technician course, and a Richland Parish site day with the college as the training face of the stop. The ask: cohort calendars, instructor presence, a named liaison. The offer: a filled top of funnel from towns the campuses do not reach, and employer tables at co-hosted events.

University of Louisiana at Lafayette

Proposal. The university's REDII Works FabLab is a free public makerspace — 3D printing, laser cutting, desktop CNC — whose roadshows already tour Acadiana towns. The tour proposes a Lafayette builder day inside the FabLab and a track feeding the Opportunity Machine incubator's builder pipeline. The ask: FabLab hosting and a staff champion. Nobody at the university has agreed to any of this yet.

University of Louisiana Monroe

Proposal. The Monroe stop's university anchor: co-hosted builder days on campus and a construction-technology showcase with the build's general contractors at their own tables. Meta's own account of the Richland build already cites this university's construction-management growth and its graduates' hiring — the tour proposes to build the community layer around that story. The ask: venue, a faculty champion, student-organisation access.

Louisiana State University — Baton Rouge

Proposal. The Baton Rouge leg's build-and-incubate home at the university's innovation park and prototyping centre: a hackathon weekend with lab access, paired with an employer day. The ask: space, a centre champion, and introductions to student founders. The offer: the tour's flagship Louisiana stop, hosted where the state's research engine already sits.

McNeese State University — Lake Charles

Proposal. The SEED Center incubator already hosts outside training and events, which makes it the natural classroom home for the southwest construction-trades rotation. The tour proposes hosted builder days and founder office hours there. The ask: hosting terms and a centre champion.

Bossier Parish Community College + Northwest Louisiana Technical Community College

Proposal. The Shreveport–Bossier training engine: boot-camp intake nights, data-centre operations tasters, and the fulfilment-automation track, co-hosted where the corridor's largest employer already runs its own intake sessions. The ask: programme calendars, instructor stations, a liaison at each college.

Baton Rouge Community College + River Parishes Community College

Proposal. The Ascension-corridor tracks — electrical and process-technology preparation aimed openly at the corridor's 2028 hiring wave, labelled as 2028 in those words. A two-year runway of prepared applicants is the one thing a 2029 plant cannot buy in 2028; the tour proposes to help build it.

Southern University + Grambling State University

Proposal. Co-hosted builder sessions in Baton Rouge, Shreveport, and the Ruston corridor, pairing these universities' labs and student teams with the stops nearest them. Partnership with the state's HBCUs is a first-class goal of this tour — stated here as intent and proposal, not as an existing relationship, because it is not one yet.

Delgado Community College + University of New Orleans

Proposal. New Orleans is the tour's digital-skills and community stop — coding sessions, startup office hours, and the community formats the season opens with — proposed plainly in those terms, with no incoming-jobs framing the evidence does not support for the city.

Ground rules, so any institution can hold the tour to them: the tour is in planning and every event is Private on Luma; no venue or sponsor is confirmed; construction, permanent, and supported job figures are never summed; a full-build-out figure is never presented as a vacancy today; and every number quoted names its source. The workforce academy's job guarantee belongs to Meta and its graduates — BuilderSpace supports applications and extends no guarantee of its own.

After Louisiana, the tour crosses to Nebraska — Omaha and Lincoln are the plan's anchor cities — for the Prairie AgTech Build Weekend, Prairie Builder Night, and Red River Pitch Night. The full phased plan is on the home page roadmap.
