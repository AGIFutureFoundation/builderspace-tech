Nine short guides on stacking startup credits with the terms stated plainly: what is actually claimable, in what order, and what quietly disqualifies you. Written from real applications, real approvals, and real rejections — the tour's second job is training new founders, and this series is where that training starts.

FREE — Amounts are providers' "up to" maximums · Confirm current terms on the provider's own page the day you apply

The plain headline, before any guide

What a pre-seed founder can realistically stack on their own is low five figures in credits and programme value. The headline numbers that circulate — $200,000 and above — are real only at tiers gated behind partners, accelerators, and referrals. Every guide in this series keeps those two numbers separate, because a plan built on the gated number fails in the first month. The sequencing logic matters as much as the amounts: three master keys open most of the map — a live website, a domain-matched email, and the right entity — and several programmes disqualify you for claiming them in the wrong order.

How to use this index: each card below is the guide in brief — what it covers and the decision it helps you make. The full guide texts are being published as resource pages; as each one lands, its card here becomes its link. The summaries on this page are the standing record until then, and the rules in every summary hold regardless: credit amounts are maximums, eligibility is the provider's call, and nothing in this series is a promise that any programme will approve any applicant.

The guides

01

The Map

The landscape, the sequencing logic, and the three master keys: a live website, a domain-matched email, the right entity. Read this first — it decides the order you claim everything else in, and getting the order wrong is the most common way founders lock themselves out of programmes they qualified for.

02

Cloud Credits

AWS, Microsoft, Google, Oracle, IBM, and DigitalOcean — the big infrastructure money. Which tiers are self-serve and which are partner-gated, what each provider counts as a startup, and who excludes nonprofits outright.

03

Infra & Dev Tools

Cloudflare, Neon, GitHub, PostHog, MongoDB, Confluent, Datadog, and friends — the tooling layer a building team actually burns through. Smaller amounts than the cloud tier, faster decisions, and the programmes most likely to say yes to a working product.

04

AI Credits

Anthropic, OpenAI, Mistral, Grok/xAI, and gateway credits — including which programmes are referral-only, what a referral actually requires, and how to keep AI spend from quietly becoming the largest line in a young budget.

05

Business Tools

Notion, HubSpot, Zendesk, Intercom, and Stripe Atlas as the incorporation on-ramp — with the onboarding-fee caution stated in full. The tools that run the company rather than the product, and the discounts that renew at full price if nobody is watching the calendar.

06

The Nonprofit Track

Goodstack, TechSoup, and the verification layer underneath both — and why being exempt by law is not the same as being verifiable in a provider's system. The track where paperwork, not product, decides the outcome.

07

When They Say No

The failure playbook: what rejection messages really mean, which ones are fixable and how to fix each one, which gates are formation, letter, or traction gates, and when the right move is to re-apply, appeal, or walk away and claim the next programme instead.

08

Redeem & Track

Dashboards, expiry tracking, good-standing hygiene, and a tracking-sheet template. Credits are perishable: the difference between a stack that funds a year of building and a stack that evaporates is a spreadsheet somebody actually maintains.
