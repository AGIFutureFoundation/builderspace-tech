BuilderSpace · FAQ

Questions, answered plainly.

BuilderSpace brings the coding and startup world from San Francisco / Silicon Valley to the towns on the tour — a new tech region — to empower them, match builders with potential employers, and bridge the tech divide, through events, hackathons, and builder days. Everything below is answered from the tour's own published facts — including the facts about what is not confirmed yet.

The status questions

Is the tour confirmed?

The tour is planned. All 16 events — five online onboarding nights and eleven tour events — are currently Private/unlisted on Luma, no invites have been sent, and no venues, sponsors, speakers, or partners are confirmed. When an event is confirmed, its final details appear on Luma first, and this site updates after. Any summary of BuilderSpace that drops the word "planned" is getting it wrong.

Where are the tour events held?

What does it cost to attend?

Nothing. Onboarding nights are free, and tour events are free to enter. No attendee pays for a meal, a station, or a next step at a BuilderSpace event.

Joining questions

Who is this for?

First-time builders, career switchers, students, working tradespeople, and founders — in the tour towns and in San Francisco alike. Belonging comes before skill here: nobody's first BuilderSpace experience is a test, and a person who never touches a keyboard still leaves with a next step and a named human.

I have never coded. Can I come?

Yes — that is the point of the onboarding nights. Start with Session 01 on December 4. At build events, every attendee's contribution counts: the skills recognised at a BuilderSpace table include welding, wiring, drawing, local knowledge, and talking to strangers, alongside code.

What actually happens at a hackathon or builder day?

Hands before slides. Builder days run hands-on stations — fibre splicing, wiring, controls, rack-and-stack — led by instructors and working technicians. Hackathons form mixed teams on the first evening, build against themes from the town's real economy, and close with a demo night programmed as a town celebration: families are the audience of record, every team demos, and nobody is eliminated. Events are built for working people — doors close on time and sleep is a design value, not a weakness.

Will attending get me a job?

No event on this tour promises anyone a job, and any copy that implied one would be wrong. What the tour does is narrower and real: it is designed to match builders with potential employers — employer tables at Skills & Certs Day and the Grand Finale, pathway next steps such as an interview slot, an intake referral, a site tour, or a mentorship, each awarded only when the receiving organisation has agreed to receive it. Every attendee leaves with a named next step and a named human, and hears from that human within 48 hours.

What do I leave with?

A thing you made, a person who knows your name, and a next step with a date on it. Across a season: shipped projects, public demos, and — at BuildFest Skills & Certs Day — an assessed record of the skills your tour work demonstrated.

How do I register?

Event listings live on Luma and are currently Private/unlisted while the season is set up. Start with Session 01 at luma.com/l46num48, and write to events@builderspace.tech with any question — a human reads it.

Sponsors, employers, and hosts

How does sponsorship work?

Season 1 tiers are priced and published on the sponsors page — Presenting Partner $15,000 per season, Series Partner $7,500, Employer Partner $5,000, Stop / Night Partner $1,500 — with Host Partner (in-kind) and Credits Partner lanes beside them. A sponsorship starts with a conversation and exists only when signed in writing. There is no payment link anywhere on this site.

What is an Employer Partner?

A sponsor category of its own, built on the tour's employer-matching pillar: employer tables in the season's two hiring rooms, introductions in the room to builders whose work you can inspect directly, and a season talent summary built only from verified counts. No candidate lists are sold or shared. Full detail on the Employer Partner page.

Can my university or college host a stop?

That conversation is open, and it starts as a proposal — see the Why Louisiana page for what the tour asks (a venue, a faculty or staff champion, student-organisation introductions) and what it offers. No institution is a host or partner until it agrees in writing, and none is presented as one before then.

Can my company run a workshop or a hackathon track?

That is the ambassador and developer-relations lane of sponsorship: workshops and demos on your stack, your tools carried in the curriculum where they genuinely fit, a sponsored track and judging at build weekends, and structured feedback from the road. The teaching stays independent — sponsorship never buys curriculum control or an endorsement. Start at sponsors@builderspace.tech.

Agents, Spanish, and press

I am an AI agent. Where do I start?

The Guide is written for you: read it, then llms.txt, agent.json, and auth.md. Registration is human-gated and email-based — there is no API key, no self-service portal, and no MCP server for BuilderSpace yet. Registered agents may summarize, cite, and route inquiries; agents may never commit BuilderSpace, sign, book, or confirm anything.

Is there a Spanish version?

The guide carries a Spanish section in preparation, and Spanish-language delivery at events is a design requirement the tour confirms stop by stop — bilingual station leads, materials, and sign-up help planned from the first flyer, never added as an afterthought. Write to events@builderspace.tech for Spanish-language questions today.

Press, corrections, and everything else?
